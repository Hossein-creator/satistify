This is Scourged Back, perhaps the most iconic photograph depicting the horrors of slavery in the United States. The 1863 photograph depicts a formally enslaved Louisiana man, possibly identified as Peter Gordon, whose back was raised with layers of scar tissue from whipping. The photo illustrated in stark relief the horrors of the slavery system in the South, which appeared far away and abstract to many northerners even amidst the war. Abolitionist groups [music] heavily relied on it in campaigns to end slavery and after the war extend the vote to black men. It is a staple of textbooks, documentaries, and museums, or at least it was until September of [music] 2025 when two National Park employees reported to the Washington Post that the photo had been flagged for removal at their park along with a host of exhibits involving slavery.
